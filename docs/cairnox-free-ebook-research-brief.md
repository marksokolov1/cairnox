# CAIRNOX free ebook: research and content readiness

Research reviewed: 5 November 2026. Planning document only; no ebook or website implementation has been created.

## Recommendation

Create a compact cricket preparation guide with one complete, Mat-approved example training week. Use the working concept **CAIRNOX: A Practical Week of Cricket Physical Preparation**. The title and the precise training emphasis still need Mat's decision.

The reader's outcome should be practical: understand the purpose of the week, place sessions around cricket, perform the approved sessions and record useful feedback. Do not promise a measurable power, speed or strength improvement in seven days.

Limit the scope through the audience, objective and time horizon. Keep enough instruction for the included week to stand alone. Exercise execution, loading, rest, suitability and schedule guidance belong in the free resource. Longer progression, individual programme adaptation, ongoing feedback and organisational implementation remain reasons to buy support.

The guide should primarily attract players. Individual coaching is the principal next step; consultancy is a smaller, clearly signposted route for coaches and organisations. A player programme by itself is unlikely to explain the full value of consultancy. A future organisational checklist could serve that audience separately.

## Source issues requiring decisions

1. **Different products:** the new document is a four-week cricket power concept with three sessions per week. The older plan is an eight-week, four-day programme with a broader strength/conditioning emphasis. They should not be blended without Mat's instruction.
2. **Missing sessions:** the new document's page 6 has no training tables or exercise prescriptions. A one-week version still needs complete session content.
3. **Duplicated day:** original pages 13 and 14 give Phase 2 Day 3 and Day 4 the same exercise and conditioning content. The rebuilt version retains this at pages 21–24. Confirm whether intentional before reuse.
4. **Season ambiguity:** the old cover says off-season, while its overview and contents refer to in-season work, early season, gaps in the schedule and support throughout the season. The free guide needs one explicit setting.
5. **Missing protocols:** Lungbuster, 500m Shuttle and Scotland Anaerobic Drill appear in the old programme without complete corresponding appendix explanations. Do not carry these names into a reader-facing sample unless Mat supplies the protocols.
6. **Undefined notation and loading:** ES / EL / EW are not defined in the old source. Some percentage-based work also needs the reference load stated explicitly for independent use.
7. **Week label:** the old Phase 1 Day 1 conditioning row repeats “Week 1” under the third week column. Confirm intent if that row is reused.
8. **New biography claims:** the six-page document describes a current “director of physical performance” role with USA Cricket and work with top-10-ranked squash players. These are more specific than the previously approved website wording. Confirm exact wording, currency and permission before publication.
9. **Claims and language:** “injury prevention” and “prehab” occur in the materials. Mat should approve the intended language and boundaries; the ebook should not imply guaranteed prevention or medical care.
10. **Missing conversion page:** the rebuilt book ends with a brand statement, without the focused ELEVATE/APEX enquiry routes requested for this free guide.

## Recommended boundary between free and paid

| Free guide | Longer independent programme / BASE | ELEVATE | APEX |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Principles, one approved example week, clear session instructions, scheduling example, log and review questions | Full approved training block and progression, with the guidance promised for that product | Individual coaching, adaptation around player/role/schedule, communication and review | Work on the organisation's planning, monitoring, delivery and staff processes |

Do not reserve rest periods, technique cues, exercise alternatives required by the selected audience, or the meaning of notation as a sales incentive. Do reserve a full later-week progression and detailed individual decisions. Do not advertise a full paid programme as purchasable until it is actually ready.

## Proposed editorial structure

Allow approximately **14–18 pages**, subject to the number of sessions Mat approves and the space needed for readable instructions. This is a proposed plan, not a draft ebook.

1. Cover: a clear cricket-specific promise and “one example training week”.
2. Start here: intended player, experience level, season context, equipment and expected use.
3. Mat and CAIRNOX: brief approved credibility statement and the purpose of physical preparation.
4. The few principles the reader needs: intent, quality and context, adapted from Mat's new material.
5. Plan the week: one approved example showing how training fits around cricket and recovery.
6. How to read and use sessions: loading, rest, grouping, notation and what to record.
7–12. Up to three approved sessions, allowing one or two pages each. Include a warm-up or clear reference to it, the exact prescription, cues and any required guidance. Mat decides the actual number and composition.
13. Adjustment guidance: what to do when the week changes, within Mat's approved boundaries.
14. Training log: record completion, loads and observations using Mat's chosen fields.
15. Review: what the reader learned and what information should shape the next step.
16. Work with Mat: principal ELEVATE route; smaller APEX route and email fallback.

Combine shorter sections or add space for exercise guidance as needed. A phone-friendly session table should show exercise, prescription, rest and cue; it does not need four week columns. Reuse the current site's typography, cream/charcoal surfaces, rules and authentic photography. Include clickable links, with QR codes only as secondary access.

If Mat cannot approve an independent sample week, use a preparation checklist and a clearly illustrative session instead. Change the title and promise accordingly. Do not advertise a week of training and deliver only principles.

## Closing conversion

The final page should help the reader choose between two needs:

- **Individual player:** apply for ELEVATE at `https://cairnox.com/contact/elevate/`.
- **Coach or organisation:** discuss APEX support at `https://cairnox.com/contact/apex/`.
- **Direct reply:** `mathew@cairnox.com`.

Mat should approve the final invitation. Keep it specific to the support available. Do not imply that downloading creates a coaching relationship or guarantees a place. The full BASE block can be mentioned as an alternative only with an accurate availability statement.

## Mailchimp journey to prepare later

Recommended flow: relevant website signup → clear confirmation → file delivery → brief helpful follow-up → appropriate coaching/project invitation.

Use a required email, optional first name, and at most one optional player/coach/organisation preference. State clearly what continuing emails the person is signing up for. Keep unrelated enquiry users separate from ebook subscribers. Mailchimp's documentation supports hosted signup forms, opt-in settings and linked file delivery rather than PDF attachments. The exact delivery method and automation availability must be checked against the owner's account and plan. [Form options](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-signup-form-options/), [opt-in settings](https://mailchimp.com/help/set-signup-preferences/), [file delivery](https://mailchimp.com/help/send-a-file-to-new-subscribers/).

Suggested internal source label: `free-cricket-guide-v1`. Keep a versioned ebook URL. Plan a repeat-download path for existing subscribers so an “already subscribed” message does not block access. Confirmation success, delivery acceptance and a click on the file are different events; test each.

If the owner chooses ongoing emails, a proportionate sequence could be immediate delivery, one practical usage email, then a reflection and coaching invitation. The cadence and content need approval; they have not been written or scheduled. Double opt-in is an option for confirming subscription, not a setting assumed to apply automatically to a custom API integration.

Evaluate confirmed subscriptions, file-link clicks, qualified ELEVATE/APEX enquiries and unsubscribes. Downloads do not prove reading or training completion. Use the first launch to establish a baseline; no conversion benchmark is assumed. Mailchimp itself recommends a specific audience, useful content and a short signup process. [Lead-magnet guidance](https://mailchimp.com/resources/what-is-a-lead-magnet/).

## Readiness decision

**Ready:** brand direction, current visual language, genuine photography, general philosophy, existing programme reference, founder experience categories and enquiry routes.

**Required from Mat before production:** reader/season definition; one approved promise; a complete sample week; loading/rest/technique/adjustment guidance; source-issue decisions for anything reused; approved biography wording; free-versus-paid boundary and final invitation.

**Required from the website owner before launch:** Mailchimp account/audience access, plan capability check, sender configuration, signup/ongoing-email wording, delivery flow and end-to-end testing. Do not send credentials in the questionnaire.

The separate Mat questionnaire captures these decisions. The ebook should be produced only after its core training content is approved.
